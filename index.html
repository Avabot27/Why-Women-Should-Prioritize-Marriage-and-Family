Why women should prioritize godly marriage and family over careers
By Ava Bot


The Problem

Society pushes the narrative that any woman who chooses marriage and motherhood submits to living an unhappy, unfulfilled, inferior life. Feminists who have advocated for equality for women in the workplace have effectively encouraged them to prioritize their careers and their education over all else. They have done this by emphasizing a woman's professional identity over her personal identity.

Second-wave feminism emerged in the 1950s in an attmpt to fix women's misery. Many women in the 50s were miserable and unfulfilled. Betty Friedan, a feminist in that time period, proposed a solution to this unhappiness: women should be able to pursue their careers and leave their families behind. She saw the domestic life as an immature life shackled to the biological role of bearing and raising children. And a husband and children were those chains that restrained women. Friedan writes, "In a sense that goes beyond any women's life, I think this is a crisis of women growing up - a turning point from an immaturity that has been called femininity to full human identity" (Feminine Mystique, 80). And many women believed her.

Now, less women are getting married than they did in the 50s. In 1949, 78.8% of US Households were headed by a married couple. But, in 2024, only 47.1% were (How has marriage in the US changed over time?). More women are joining the workforce. According to the U.S. Department of Labor, 57.2% of women participate in the workforce. In the 1960s, only 22% of women did (Labor Status of Women & Men). Rebekah Merkle, in Eve in Exile, writes, "It seems safe to say that in America today, women have the freedom to pursue careers if they so choose. And they usually so choose. . . There is no doubt that women today are less tied to motherhood than they were in the 50s (65-66). So, if Friedan was right, the overall quality of life for women should be improving. Careers should be fixing their misery.


The Solution


Women should prioritize godly marriage and family over their careers because these things fulfill God's role for women better, make women healthier, and make women happier. I am not saying that women should not get married, nor that they should not have jobs, but that if they are called to marriage and motherhood, their priorities should be family first, careers second.


Godly Marriage and Family Fulfill Women's God-given Role Better


Women should prioritize godly marriage and family over careers because these things fulfill God's role for women. Scripture alludes to this role through the creation of Adam and Eve, through the ideal Proverbs 31 woman, through Adam and Eve's fall, and through Christ and the Church.

First, God created Eve to be a helper comparable to Adam, her husband, and to cultivate life through bearing children. Although they have different roles, men and women were created by God with equal ontological value, or equal value of being. Genesis 1:27 states, "So God created man in His own image. . . male and female He created them" (New King James Version). God calls Eve "a helper comparable" to Adam, because "it is not good that man should be alone" (Gen. 2.18). She was created, not to be another Adam, but to be with Adam, as a helper. And she fulfills this role through marriage. Just as it was not good that man be alone, neither is it good for women. God bestowed upon women the special ability to cultivate life through childbearing. This is no small responsibility. Adam could not fulfill God's command to fill and subdue the earth without a wife, without Eve. And she does this through marriage and having children. So, in order to fulfill the role that God created women for, women should prioritize marriage and family.

Second, in Proverbs 31, the worth of a virtuous wife "is far above rubies" (v. 10). And what does this godly woman do? She cares for her house and children. She helps her spouse. She fulfills her created role. This virtuous woman still labors for her household. Verse 16 states, "She considers a field and buys it; from her profits she plants a vineyard." This goes to show that women can and should have jobs. But many women prioritize careers to work for their own sake, whether they want endless glory like Achilles or wealth to rival that of Croesus. But, all that the Proverbs 31 woman does, she does for the sake of her family. And to work for the sake of family, a woman first must have a family. So, it is not right for women to sacrifice starting a family in order to work.

Third, even in the Curse, God still outlines the role of women. God did not curse working the ground for Eve. He cursed childbearing because it is her area of responsibility. It is her most important role that was given at creation.

Fourth, God created marriage as a way to imitate Christ's love for the church. Kevin DeYoung writes in Men and Women in the Church, "Marriage is a picture of Christ and the church. If there is no distinction in how we relate to each, no ordering, no self-sacrificing headship, no joyful submission, we are left with Christ and Christ or the church and the church. . . If we disallow sexual differentiation, we are not allowing to shine forth the very heart of marriage itself" (74). This idea comes from Ephesians 5:25, which states, "Husbands, love you wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her." God created the roles in marriage to illustrate His glory to the world, to imitate Christ and the church. Women should not take on man's created role, and neither should man take on woman's role. When women spurn the idea of marriage and family for their own sake, they dishonor God. In order to honor God and to recognize Him as Creator, women should prioritize marriage and family over careers. When they do work, it should be to provide for their family, not to gain glory for themselves.


Godly Marriage and Family Make Women Mentally and Physically Healthier


Women should prioritize godly marriage and family over careers because these things make women mentally and physically healthier in two ways: they maintain better lifestyles and have a built-in support system to combat depression.

First, those who are married tend to make better health choices. They usually take fewer risks, eat healthier, and go to the doctor more than single people (Shmerling). An article from Purdue University states that "Married men and married women live, on average, two years longer than their unmarried counterparts. One reason for this longevity benefit is the influence of marital partners on healthy behaviors. Study after study shows that married people eat better and are less likely to smoke and drink excessively" (Franks, Richards, Shrout). They generally make better choices either at the request of their spouse or for the sake of being there for their spouse, leading to a longer and healthier life.

Second, marriage provides a built-in partner to be there when things go wrong. So, married people generally succumb less to threats to their mental health, such as depression, anxiety, and loneliness, because of the support and loving companionship that a godly marriage offers (Shmerling). The Institute for Family Studies found that "marriage seems to confer its mental health benefits indiscriminately; that is, members of nearly every major demographic group in American society are better off from a mental health perspective relative to their unmarried peers." No matter the age, race, gender, wealth, or education, married people tend to have better mental health. 17-18% more married people report good mental health than unmarried people (Wallsten). Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 agrees with this, stating "Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor. For if they fall, one will lift up his companion. But woe to him who is alone when he falls, for he has no one to help him up." This passage talks of the value of a friend. And what better friend is there than one's spouse? When something goes wrong, that companion, who promised to be there through the bad times and the good, will lift the other up, effectively thwarting threats to their partner's mental health. Although some marriages can be more harmful to a person's health than single life, typically a godly marriage, where each partner loves the other as themselves, can lengthen life and improve its quality (Eph. 5.33). So, in order to maintain good health, women should rank a godly, loving marriage above their careers.

Third, as a general rule, married people have stronger immune functions, which defend the body against viruses, bacteria, and toxins. Married people tend to be less stressed because they have a dependable spouse who is with them through the bad times and the good. Researchers from Carnegie Melon University found that, after studying 572 adults, ages 21 to 55, married people had much lower cortisol levels than single people and "Consistently high cortisol levels, which can be caused by ongoing stress, can interfere with the body's ability to regulate inflammation. . . Inflammation has been linked to many health conditions, including heart problems, lower immunity, diabetes, and cancer" (MacMillan). Although many things can cause and worsen anxiety, a godly marriage provides a spouse who can comfort and give aid, lessening this level of anxiety.


Godly Marriage and Family Make Women Happier


Women should prioritize godly marriage and family over careers because these things tend to make women happier. This truth can be seen in antidepressants statistics and in the Bible.

First, Merkle presents these statistics in her book Eve in Exile. Happiness is difficult to measure, but antidepressant statistics can accurately reflect levels of happiness to some extent. She writes, "In 1963, one in every five women was medicating her unhappiness, and today we're up to more than one in four women. And women are more than two and a half times more likely to be on medications than men" (69). The number of women in the workplace has increased 35% since the 50s, so, if Friedan was right, eliminating sexual differentiation should have caused a decrease in women on antidepressants, not an increase. Although antidepressants have become more accessible than they were in the 50s, if careers truly bring happiness, less women should need to medicate their misery. The role of men in society has changed little since the 50s. Most men join the workforce, just as they have always done. If women are getting the equality they have always wanted, why are they two and half times more likely to take antidepressants than men? [Interrogatio]. When women spurn their God-given role to take on a man's, of course they are unhappier. Many things can cause depression, but with so many women prioritizing careers over family and insisting that it will make them happy, there should be evidence to support this claim. Instead, the evidence contradicts it.

Second, Scripture also tells us that a spouse brings blessedness. Proverbs 18:22 states, "He who finds a wife finds a good thing, and obtains favor from the LORD." Although Scripture talks of the man who finds a good wife, the same is true for women. She who finds a husband finds a good thing, and obtains favor from the Lord. The husband and children of the Proverbs 31 woman even call her blessed and praise her. So, a godly marriage leads a woman to blessedness.

Third, Scripture treats children as a blessing from the Lord. Psalm 127:3-5 states, "Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb is a reward. Like arrows in the hand of a warrior, so are the children of one's youth. Happy is the man who has a quiver full of them." The fruit of the womb is a reward for both husband and wife, that it might bring them happiness. According to Scripture, building a family is not a burden or chain keeping a woman from attaining full human identity, like Friedan thought, but a reward. And God has given women the honor of carrying and cultivating this reward. Verse 5 of Psalm 127 clearly states that one who has many children, or "a quiver full," is happy. Does the Bible ever say, "Happy is the self-dependent women who does not need a man?" No, it does not. Just as the fruit of the womb was given as a reward, so was a barren womb given as a curse. In the Old Testament, Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah, and Elizabeth were all barren. They wept and prayed to God for children, because the inability to have children was viewed as a curse. When God finally gave them children, they all praised Him and were happy. Not only does God outline childbearing as a blessing, and barrenness as a curse, but He also issues a command to have children in the creation mandate. A command of God, such as having children, brings joy, not sorrow. Everyone wants to be happy, so women should choose things that bring about happiness, like a godly marriage and children, even if it means sacrificing their careers.


Common Objections


Some people might say that the domestic life of cultivating house and family is an inferior role to that of the working man. They think that the Biblical role of woman is to submit to men. However, the role of women is not to submit to men. Neither is the Biblical role of women inferior to man's role.

First, caring for the house and family is not inferior to working a nine-to-five job. Merkle writes that "The 50s idealized notion of a woman who only exists to look pretty and make the house look pretty is just as much a revolt against creation order as the radical feminist agenda is - it's just a revolt against a different part. . . forgetting what it was Eve was created for" (102). Many people think that the Bible promotes this useless woman of the 50s, whose only job is to have children and look good for her husband. But this is not Biblical. Eve and the Proverbs 31 woman never sat around with nothing to do like mere trophy wives. They worked. Eve, like Adam, worked in the garden, just in a different way. As mentioned earlier, the godly woman in Proverbs also provides for her family, just in a different way. She provides food, buys a field, plants a vineyard, gives to the poor, and makes and sells clothes (15-24). She does so much more than just sit around at home waiting for her husband. Her job is equally as important as his, it just has a different emphasis.

Second, the creation mandate also regards each role equally. Both sexes take dominion over the earth. Both fill and subdue it. They go about it in different ways, because both approaches are necessary. If every woman decided to focus on nothing but work, humans would soon die out. If every man decided to stay at home, many jobs would remain undone. Just as man and woman were created equally, so were their roles. Society cannot function without both sexes doing their part.

Third, the Bible never states that women should submit to men. Ephesians 5:22 states, "Wives, submit to your husbands, as to the Lord." This submission does not mean that, when married, women somehow become inferior to men. It is actually the glory of wives to submit to their husbands. Merkle writes, "that submission itself is what is so glorious, and that is because the willing submission of one equal to another - a submission offered out of love and not out of servitude - is a submission that pictures Christ" (120). By submitting to their husbands, women mirror Christ. Taking on Adam's role is what led Eve to eat the forbidden fruit in the first place. It was her fatal flaw, her hamartia. And taking on man's role continues to be women's forbidden fruit, like it was for Eve. Just as Christ, although equal with the Father, submitted to Him and gained glory, so also do wives, when they submit to their husbands, gain glory. To echo DeYoung and Merkle, if there is no joyful submission or self-sacrificing headship between equals in a marriage, the glory is lost. So, neither the role of women, nor women themselves, are inferior to men. Although the emphases of their roles differ, their importance remain equal.

Some people argue that women can get married and raise children after they establish their careers, however; women who do this have a much lower chance of getting married and having children.

First, women who prioritize careers over marriage are less likely to find a good spouse. By 2030, "45% of prime working age women (ages 25-44) will be single" (Stanley). In an article by Jennifer Sey, she writes, "by delaying marriage indefinitely these women wound up with fewer choices. Good men of strong character, who are conscientious and gainfully employed, tend to marry younger, family-oriented women. . . Ergo, by the time the career focused women are around 30 years of age, the pickings are slim" (On prioritizing family over career). After a woman postpones marriage, many men are either already taken or looking for younger women. Researchers found that, as they get older, men, if they are not already married or in a serious relationship, tend to marry women younger than them. They found that “men report maximum acceptable partner ages that hover around their own age through their 40s. After 40, maximum age preferences for most categories remain lower than their own age" (DiDonato). Women who prioritize their career over marriage might start to look for a husband in their mid-thirties or forties, after they have established themselves in their job. Most men their age will likely already be married, be looking for someone younger, or will be divorced with children. Since it is harder to get married after establishing a career, it follows that it would be harder to have children also.

Not only do women's likelihood of getting married decrease when they prioritize careers before marriage, but their likelihood of having children does also. American women are much more likely to have children when they are married (Abbamonte). So, since the number of U.S. households headed by a married couple have decreased around 32% since 1949, so has the nation's fertility rate. USA Facts states that "The nation's fertility rate has declined since 1957, when there were 122.9 births per 1,000 women. . . Data for 2023 puts the current fertility rate at less than half the 1957 rate, 54.5" (How have US fertility and birth rates changed over time?). The Heritage Foundation agrees with this, writing in an article that, "much of the recent decline in fertility is a result of women postponing births. . . The delay in marriage has reduced women's window of opportunity to decide to have children with a stable and reliable partner." Jonathon Abbamonte, the author of this article, proposes that the reason for the delay in marriage "could be due to today's young adults having priorities other than marriage or wanting to attain certain accomplishments, such as education, earnings, or a career before getting married." Because many young adults, especially women, have been pushed to seek a career over marriage, marriage and fertility rates have dropped. Women who wait to get married and have children are much less likely to do these things.


Conclusion


So, women should prioritize marriage and family over their careers because marriage leads to a more faithful, fulfilled, healthier, and happier life. If women continue to prioritize careers over marriage and family, if they continue to look at married life with scorn, they will only harm their relationship with their Creator, their own health, happiness, and future chances of getting married and having children. To quote DeYoung, "[God] created marriage to reveal this profound mystery of Christ and the church. This is a high calling. . . Nothing less than God's glory is at stake" (DeYoung, 74). So, do not let anything stop you from glorifying God, because glorifying Him is what you were created to do.
